Product

The building blocks of a working data business.

Six modules, each targeting a critical layer, and each one ending in something built, not recommended. Combined, they create an operating architecture that holds together in the market.

  1. Advisory and review of Master Service Agreements (MSA), order forms, and commercial terms governing data licensing and delivery. Aligns contractual language with monetisation best practice — including modern use cases such as API distribution, downstream access or consumption to derived data, and AI-driven applications with clearly defined permissioning tiers.

    What's included

    • MSA & order form clause review with redline recommendations
    • Trial Agreement review and alignement on permitted workflows
    • AI-use permissioning matrix across data tiers
    • Redistribution & downstream use framework
    • Audit-ready entitlement model

  2. Design and assessment of pricing frameworks and commercial models (seat-based, usage-based, enterprise, embedded). Aligns pricing with packaging, segmentation, and real client workflows, from direct data consumption to advanced integration use cases, ensuring models scale as customer utilisation and value creation evolve.

    What's included

    • Pricing framework design across seat / usage / enterprise / embedded
    • Bespoke workflow rights aligned onto transfer of IP and corresponding premium
    • Segmentation-driven pricing tiers with elasticity assumptions
    • Three-year revenue projection scenarios
    • Pricing model, and the pricing engine that runs it: a working tool your team quotes from, not a static spreadsheet
    • Discounting guardrails & commercial governance

  3. Strategic input on long-term product development — including prioritisation of data assets, API improvements, and delivery channels to support future scalability and monetisation. Translates business ambition into a sequenced delivery plan with clear trade-offs and dependencies.

    What's included

    • Roadmap outline with quarterly sequencing
    • Asset prioritisation matrix (impact × effort × dependency)
    • Trade-off framework for build / buy / partner decisions
    • Delivery planning & resource modelling

  4. Product positioning and value proposition design. Includes review or creation of pitch decks and go-to-market narratives that equip Sales and Pre-Sales teams to articulate the commercial and strategic value of data products to specific buyer personas.

    What's included

    • Pitch deck refinement (cover, problem, solution, proof, CTA)
    • Persona-mapped value propositions with proof points
    • Sales enablement playbook & objection-handling guide
    • Discovery question library by buyer role

  5. Review or creation of data packaging strategy, ensuring alignment with customer workflows, investment strategies, and commercial models. Covers asset-type bundles, regional groupings, and thematic or cross-asset packages calibrated for clear price/value laddering.

    What's included

    • Tiered package architecture
    • Asset-type bundles aligned to buyer workflows
    • Entity Mastery evaluation
    • Thematic cross-asset packages
    • Data Dictionary & Content Methodology guidance
    • Data-driven evaluation of packaging options

  6. Technical and functional advisory on data delivery channels — APIs, file-based delivery, marketplace listings. Includes endpoint specifications, recommended delivery workflows, and architecture for listing on platforms such as Snowflake or Databricks.

    What's included

    • OpenAPI endpoint specifications & field-level documentation (pre-requisite for MCP servers)
    • Snowflake / Databricks marketplace listing specifications
    • Delivery workflow architecture (real-time / batch / hybrid)
    • Latency & SLA framework with monitoring approach
Easter egg

You've been curious — and patient.

Clicking the logo does trigger something. Here's what's actually going on across the dial:

Each dot travelling between modules on the dial is one interaction weight — a moment where two modules influence each other in real engagements. The denser the dot traffic between two modules, the more they tend to iterate together in practice.

  • The more we iterate on Monetisation Model & Pricing, the more we have to retrain the Sales team — so dots flow heavily from there into Value Strategy & Enablement.
  • Offer Design & Packaging usually precedes the pricing engine, which is why those two share a constant back-and-forth.
  • And the pricing engine itself is built on the back of the licensing terms and workflow rights agreed upstream — so dots cascade in from Rights & Licensing.

It's not random. It's the choreography of how the modules actually fit together.
